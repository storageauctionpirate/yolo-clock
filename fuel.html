Your setup

Strength training 2+ days/week

Protein only builds muscle when muscles are asked to work. US activity guidelines advise muscle-strengthening activity on 2 or more days a week. See what it may add on your Life Clock, or open Look it up and search “strength”.

How the targets are worked out ▾

Protein: 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day (about 0.7 g per lb). A 2018 review of 49 trials found extra protein stopped adding muscle gains from strength training at around 1.62 g/kg/day (Morton et al., Br J Sports Med 2018).

Per meal: about 0.4 g/kg per meal over at least 4 meals reaches that daily amount (Schoenfeld & Aragon, J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2018).

Calories: resting burn by the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, times your activity level. Build muscle: +250 to +500 kcal. Lose fat: −500 kcal. Maintain: no change.

Fat: 25–30% of calories. Carbs: the rest.

These are estimates, not lab measurements. Your real needs may differ.

Macros & micros, plainly ▾

Protein (4 kcal/g) — the building blocks for muscle repair and growth. Found in meat, fish, eggs, dairy, beans, tofu.

Carbs (4 kcal/g) — the main fuel for hard training. Found in rice, oats, bread, potatoes, fruit.

Fat (9 kcal/g) — needed for hormones and to absorb vitamins A, D, E, K. Found in oils, nuts, avocado, fatty fish.

Micros are vitamins and minerals, needed in small amounts. Vitamin D and calcium support bones; iron carries oxygen in the blood; magnesium helps muscles and nerves work; potassium helps muscles contract and supports healthy blood pressure.

Daily micro targets: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheets (RDA / Adequate Intake for adults).

Not medical advice. Estimates for healthy adults only. If you have kidney disease, talk to your doctor before eating more protein. Pregnant, under 18, or managing a health condition? Ask a doctor or dietitian first.
